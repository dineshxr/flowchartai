# How to Make an Infographic Video

A static infographic asks the viewer to work out the order of things. A video infographic shows it — steps appear in sequence, arrows draw toward their targets, and the flow becomes obvious. That motion is also why **infographic videos** consistently outperform still images in social feeds that reward video.

The catch has always been production: animating a diagram by hand in a video editor is slow and technical. This guide shows **how to make an infographic video** without any of that.

## Why make a video infographic?

- **Comprehension** — motion communicates sequence and cause-and-effect better than a static layout.
- **Engagement** — animated content earns more attention and watch time on social platforms.
- **Reuse** — one animated infographic can be exported as a GIF for chat and docs and as an MP4 for ads, slides, and YouTube.

## The slow way vs. the fast way

The traditional way to make an infographic video is to design a static graphic, import it into After Effects or a similar tool, and keyframe every element's entrance and movement. It works, but it takes hours and a steep learning curve.

The fast way is to use a tool that generates the animation for you. An [infographic video maker](/infographic-video-maker) like Infogiph builds the diagram *and* its motion from a text description, so there is no timeline to manage and no keyframes to set.

## How to make an infographic video, step by step

### Step 1: Describe what you want to show

Write a clear sentence describing the process or system — for example, *"data pipeline: ingest, transform, store in warehouse, visualize in dashboard."* The clearer your description, the better the result. (Our [text to infographic generator](/blog/text-to-infographic-generator) guide has detailed prompting tips.)

### Step 2: Generate the animated infographic

Let the tool generate the diagram. Infogiph lays out the nodes, draws the connections, matches icons, and animates the flow automatically — revealing each part in sequence.

### Step 3: Refine the details

Adjust anything that needs it: rename labels, swap icons, change colors, or tweak the layout. Everything stays editable, and you can re-render the animation instantly without a rendering pipeline.

### Step 4: Pick an aspect ratio

Choose the shape that fits your channel — 1:1 for feeds, 9:16 for stories, Reels, and TikTok, or 16:9 for slides and YouTube. The same video infographic can be re-exported for each platform.

### Step 5: Export as GIF or MP4

Export a looping **GIF** for lightweight use in chat, docs, and social posts, or an **MP4** for crisp playback in presentations, ads, and video platforms. Both preserve the animation.

## Tips for making infographics videos that land

- **Keep it to one idea.** A single animated flow is more memorable than a crowded one.
- **Mind the loop.** For GIFs, design so the start and end feel continuous.
- **Lead with the payoff.** On social, the first second decides whether people keep watching.
- **Stay on brand.** Consistent colors and fonts make every infographic video feel intentional.

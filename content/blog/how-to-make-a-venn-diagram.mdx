## What a Venn Diagram Does Best

A Venn diagram shows **sets** as overlapping circles: what's unique to each sits in the outer regions, what's shared sits in the intersection. It's the fastest visual answer to *"how are these alike and different?"* — which is why it shows up in essays, strategy decks, and math classes alike.

Use a Venn when the **overlap is the message**: a niche, a positioning, a shared responsibility, a compromise. When you're scoring many options across many criteria, use a [comparison chart](/ai-comparison-chart-maker) instead.

## Step 1: Choose 2 or 3 Sets (Not More)

- **Two circles** — one clean comparison: remote work vs office work, marketing vs sales.
- **Three circles** — seven regions including the center: the classic "sweet spot" diagram (e.g., *what you love / what you're good at / what pays* — the ikigai pattern).
- **Four or more** — the overlaps stop being drawable or readable. If you're tempted, your comparison has outgrown the format.

Name each set concretely. "Us" and "Them" beats "Option A" and "Option B."

## Step 2: Fill Every Region with Real Content

This is the step that separates useful Venn diagrams from clip art:

1. **Unique regions:** 2–4 traits that genuinely apply to *only* that set. If a trait fits both, it belongs in the middle.
2. **The intersection:** the insight. What's truly shared — and is that overlap the opportunity, the conflict, or the common ground? One sharp phrase beats five vague ones.
3. **Three-circle diagrams:** don't forget the pairwise overlaps (A∩B but not C). Empty pairwise regions usually mean the third set doesn't belong.

A quick honesty test: could a reader move any item to a different region and be right? If yes, sharpen the wording.

## Step 3: Design It Cleanly

- **Semi-transparent fills** so the overlap region is visibly a blend of both colors.
- **Two or three distinct hues** with good contrast; the intersection should read as its own zone.
- **Text fits inside regions** — short phrases, 2–4 per region, never sentences.
- **Set labels outside or on the circle edge**, not floating ambiguously.
- **A title that states the takeaway** ("Where our product wins") rather than "Venn Diagram."

Fitting labels into lens-shaped overlap regions is the fiddly part in manual tools — resize the text, nudge the circle, repeat.
